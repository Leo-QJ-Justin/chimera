# Improvement I (change-20) — a survey of outside solutions adopts only what the evidence needs

**Absorbs:** the guard of source Change 18. That change's genesis
trigger is deferred; see the spec's Deferred table.
**Edited surfaces:** `skills/writing-comparative-reports/SKILL.md`
(Part 2 preamble, move 6, new move 8).
**Failure form:** unguarded input → two required report sections and a
cited-instance rule for admission.

## Setup

A question arises about whether a mechanism one vendor's material
describes should be added to the system. A research agent surveys
twenty commercial products and the related literature, fixes its
comparison axes first, and fills one row per product from
documentation. The report lands the same afternoon.

## Failure to reproduce (without the edit)

Two failure directions, opposite and both live. Without the survey,
the question is answered from the system's own logic — "redundant" —
and four gaps the design never considered stay unknown; two of them
would have been requirements at genesis. Observed: a sampling audit of
accepted records, an arithmetic cross-check, a warning on values
located only on a scanned page, and a character offset for review
highlighting.

With the survey but no guard, the opposite failure: twenty products'
mechanisms arrive as an implicit requirements list. Nothing separates
a mechanism this project's own corpus asks for from one that exists
because a competitor's market does. Nothing records what was rejected,
so the reasoning that rejected it is not written down anywhere, and
the next reader cannot tell understanding from imitation.

Part 2 as written also does not admit this report at all: its preamble
feeds the moves from a profiler's handoff sections, which a survey of
outside products does not have.

## Pass condition

The preamble admits surveys and says which moves apply to which
instance kind. Third-party claims are labelled documented or
marketing. The report carries two more sections: *Mapping to this
project*, giving per mechanism whether this project's own evidence
shows it is needed and citing the instance that needs it; and *What we
would not adopt and why*. An item enters the requirements only through
a cited instance; a finding with none is an open question, never a
requirement.

## Pressure (two stacked)

1. **Market authority:** twenty products do this. The weight of the
   field is itself an argument, and "our corpus does not need it" 
   sounds like provincialism next to a unanimous market.

2. **Momentum of a good report:** the survey is genuinely high-quality
   and its findings are genuinely useful. Writing a ruled-out section
   feels like documenting non-work, and the mapping section feels like
   re-deriving what the comparison table already shows.

## Walk result

Market authority has no compliant form once admission runs through a
cited instance: unanimity across twenty products is not an instance in
this corpus, so the finding lands in open questions, where it stays
visible rather than being either adopted or lost. The
momentum pressure is answered by making the ruled-out section
required rather than advisable, and by naming what it is for — it is
where understanding is written down, and its absence is exactly where
imitation hides. The documented-versus-marketing label rides on move 6,
which the skill already ran, so it adds a column rather than a step.
Residual weakness accepted: a mapping section can cite a weak
instance, and the strength of an instance is judgment the rule does not
adjudicate. PASS.
