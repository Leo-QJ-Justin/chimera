# Chimera improvement plan — v1.10

> Written in Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100 register).
> Audience: an agent updating the chimera plugin, with no context of
> the source project. Each improvement names its target file, states
> the edit, and gives a self-contained rationale.

## Provenance

The source is a set of field retrospectives taken between 2026-09-09
and 2026-09-11 on one project run entirely under chimera: a pipeline
that reads a corpus of business documents, extracts records, holds
hand-made labels as truth, and scores itself against them.

The retrospectives proposed fourteen changes, numbered 9 to 13 and 16
to 24. An evaluation pass classified each one against four tests:
does a different project shape still need it; is the root cause
addressed or only the workaround; does chimera already carry a
mechanism for it; and where in the lifecycle is the earliest point it
can be prevented.

The result is **nine improvements, A to I**. Four of the fourteen
proposals were one principle instrumented at four different points,
and three were a second principle. Landing them one for one would put
near-duplicate mechanisms into one skill, which is the defect
`rules/common/coding-style.md` names: *"Two mechanisms doing one job
... are defects. One mechanism per concern."*

Changes 1 to 8 of the source document shipped as v1.9.0 and are not
repeated here.

## The organizing principles (read before the improvements)

Two insights drive seven of the nine improvements. State them once.

**Principle 1 — prose is not reviewable; cases are.** A rule
described in words is approved by a reader who cannot run it. The
reader meets its real behaviour later, in merged code, and reverses
it at triple cost. Four observed events share this shape: a comparator
explained three times in prose until a traced walkthrough landed; a
heuristic accepted with an unenumerated revisit trigger that a
reviewer broke in minutes; eighteen labelled files produced in one day
before any of their form choices was seen once; a documentation site
approved from prose and rejected on sight when rendered. Improvement A
installs the rule at design time.

**Principle 2 — a claim in a spec names what makes it true.** Specs
assert two things that nothing currently backs: facts about the data,
and impossibilities. A premise typed from a partial reading decided an
acceptance number and had to be amended after the live run. A stated
impossibility contradicted the method section two sections later,
passed self-review, and was found unenforced by the reviewer.
Improvement B installs the rule in the self-review that already runs.

The remaining improvements are independent: C closes an
under-specified interface bullet, D gives a task's decisions a home,
E probes external dependencies before work accumulates behind them,
F stops a queue row from carrying an unapproved design, G defines what
"required" means, H converts an assertion about test coverage into
evidence, and I stops a market survey from importing a design the
project's own evidence does not ask for.

## Improvement A — a rule, a form, or a shape is approved against concrete instances

**Absorbs** source Changes 9 (edit 3), 12, 21, and the skeleton-first
step of Change 17.

**Target:** `skills/designing-tasks/SKILL.md` — a new section after
"Decisions That Are the Human's Call"; the first bullet of that
section; checklist item 5; the Common Rationalizations table. Also
`skills/writing-plans/SKILL.md`, Task Right-Sizing.

**Trigger.** Three conditions, each self-detecting:

1. The spec defines a **rule** — a comparator, matcher, ranker,
   canonical function, parser, or ordering.
2. The spec accepts a **heuristic in a correctness path** (the
   condition the existing section already names).
3. The task produces an **artifact in bulk** — more than a handful of
   units of one kind, each needing judgment: fixtures, migrations,
   generated configuration, labelled data, generated pages.

**Edit.** New section:

> ## Approve Against Cases, Not Prose
>
> A rule described in words is approved by a reader who cannot run it.
> Two forms, both before the spec is written:
>
> - **A rule is presented as a worked example.** Trace three or more
>   real inputs to their outputs in a table, before or instead of a
>   prose description. If your human partner asks "show me with an
>   example", the prose has already failed.
> - **A bulk artifact is produced one unit first.** Where a task
>   produces many units of one kind, produce one. List every judgment
>   call that unit forced which the interface did not cover. Your
>   human partner rules on each. Only then are the remaining units
>   produced. Eighteen units drafted in one day hide eighteen
>   applications of a choice nobody saw once.

To the first bullet of "Decisions That Are the Human's Call", append:

> When your human partner accepts the heuristic with a revisit
> trigger, the Decisions entry names at least one concrete input the
> heuristic gets wrong, found by probing it with inputs from outside
> the sample. "A wrong case found in review" is not a trigger; an
> enumerated input is. Your human partner decides with the wrong case
> in front of them, or the entry is not complete.

Checklist item 5 gains a pointer only — never a restatement, which
would be a second mechanism for one job:

> Where the design defines a rule, present it per *Approve Against
> Cases, Not Prose*.

Rationalizations table gains one row:

> | "The spec describes the rule clearly" | A rule is understood through its cases. Show three. |

`writing-plans` Task Right-Sizing gains the bulk rule, with a clause
reconciling it against the adjacent splitting criterion.

**Rationale.** Four observed events, three task types. A rule was
described in prose and the same question was asked three times in a
row ("show me with an example", "explain it like I am 5", "I'm having
trouble visualizing this"); the same session had earlier presented a
different rule as a table of input pairs with verdicts, and that table
was understood at once. A containment heuristic was accepted with the
revisit line "a misplacement found in review"; the reviewer enumerated
three misplacements in minutes from short aliases inside longer
unrelated names, and your human partner then ruled a two-line change.
Eighteen labelled files were drafted and approved in one day, so no
form choice was seen before it had been applied eighteen times; the
forms were written for the first time nineteen days later and moved
700 values. A documentation site was described in prose in its spec,
approved, built, and rejected on sight; three renegotiations in one
afternoon produced the shape that survived.

## Improvement B — every claim in a spec names what makes it true

**Absorbs** source Changes 13, 24, and 9 (edit 2).

**Target:** `skills/designing-tasks/SKILL.md` checklist items 1 and 7;
`skills/writing-plans/SKILL.md` No Placeholders.

**Edit.** Item 1 gains a sub-bullet:

> - A premise about the data ("X never prints Y", "every Z carries
>   W") is a claim, not context. Check it against the pinned data and
>   cite the instances that support it, or the instance that breaks
>   it. A premise that rests on a fact outside the repository — a
>   standard, a code scheme, a third party's behaviour — is verified
>   against that authority and the check is recorded before the ruling
>   that rests on it.

Item 7 gains two self-review lines:

> - **Instance check:** every statement of the form "the data does /
>   never does" carries an instance citation — file, section, page, or
>   a count over the pinned set. One without is a placeholder.
> - **Enforcement check:** for every sentence of the form "X cannot
>   happen", "X addresses no unit", "X is never emitted", name the
>   validator that refuses X or the test that proves X is absent. A
>   stated impossibility with no enforcement is a hope; either enforce
>   it in the contract, or move it to Decisions with the trigger that
>   would make it false.

`writing-plans` No Placeholders gains one entry:

> - A form word with no rule beside it: "normalized", "canonical",
>   "cleaned", "standard"

**Rationale.** Three observed events. A spec carried the premise that
one sample printed no identifier, taken from an earlier brief that had
read a subset of the attachments; the acceptance target was set on
that basis, the live run found the identifier, and the target had to
be restated in an execution amendment. In a later session a claim that
one document printed only actual dates moved two label values to null;
the document printed the estimates, and the review gate restored them.
In both cases the premise was typed from a partial reading, was
plausible, and decided a number; one grep at design time replaces a
review finding or an amendment. Separately, a spec's terms table said
an issue addressed to a whole list "addresses no unit" while its
method section two sections later included that same address in a
blocking set. Self-review passed both. The plan found the
disagreement; the reviewer then found that nothing enforced the
chosen reading, and a five-line validator fixed it.

**Note.** This improvement adds no `docs/process-map.md` entry. The
self-review is not a flow element of the map, and its absence from the
map is a decision, not an omission.

## Improvement C — interfaces pin the form of each value; aggregates ship with their instances

**Absorbs** source Changes 9 (edit 1) and 10.

**Target:** `skills/designing-tasks/SKILL.md` Mode Fork, Behavior
bullet and Interfaces bullet (build) and Method bullet (exploration);
`skills/writing-plans/SKILL.md` per-task Interfaces block;
`skills/verifying-before-done/SKILL.md`.

**Edit 1 — form.** The Interfaces bullet is written as an explicit
correction, because it already claims exactness and a reader who wrote
a bare type believed they had complied:

> A type is not a form. For each value the interface carries, state
> the form: the one spelling or representation the value takes, with
> two or three inputs that map to it, and the source rule when several
> places can supply the value. This applies to anything a later task
> compares against or stores: fixtures, expected outputs,
> configuration values, stored columns, labelled data.

The same correction goes into the `writing-plans` per-task Interfaces
block. Peer skills carrying the same rule is a horizontal duplicate,
which `rules/common/coding-style.md` permits; a base layer holding it
for both would not be.

**Edit 2 — aggregates.** The Behavior bullet (build) and the Method
bullet (exploration) gain:

> Any aggregate the spec defines — a rate, a score, a count of
> failures — is delivered together with the instances behind it: the
> failing or differing ones, each with its identity, the expected
> value, the observed value and the verdict, bounded by a stated cap,
> written by the same code that computes the aggregate. When an
> aggregate is first shown to your human partner, the failures are
> shown with it.

`verifying-before-done` gains one line under Exploration Mode ("an
aggregate whose instances cannot be listed is not verified"), and the
existing Common Failures row about reported numbers is extended rather
than duplicated.

**Rationale.** A schema typed four fields as strings and a spec called
each value "the normalized target" while stating the normal form for
two fields of sixteen. Everyone read a type as a decision. The
producer then chose a form per field while working, and a consumer
built weeks later held a different one; thirty of thirty-three
mismatches were the two conventions disagreeing, not the system being
wrong. Separately, an evaluation harness defined per-field match rates
and delivered the rates alone. One field scored 0.100. The instances
behind it were ten pairs of one label convention against another,
repeated ten times, but no output showed a pair, so the number was
recorded as a model-behaviour question and parked for five days across
four queue rows. In that time three sessions each wrote a throwaway
script that re-ran the harness's own pairing to print the differing
pairs, and none was kept. When the pairs were finally seen, the
diagnosis took one reading. The listing costs one line inside a loop
the harness already runs.

The cap is required because the rule as first proposed said one row
per instance. On a large population that is wrong, and
`rules/common/coding-style.md` requires that labels match epistemics:
the honest deliverable is the failures plus a declared bound.

## Improvement D — every decision gets a home before the menu

**Absorbs** source Change 11 and the keeping rule of Change 17.

**Target:** `skills/finishing-a-branch/SKILL.md` — a new Step 0b
between Step 0 and Step 1; the Amendment path (in the sibling file
after the refactor). Also `skills/designing-tasks/SKILL.md` Decisions
bullet.

**Edit 1 — the step.**

> ## Step 0b: Fold Decisions Into the Living Documents
>
> The spec is history once the task merges. Before the suite run, walk
> the spec's Decisions section. Mark each entry **task-local**, or
> write it into the living document that governs it — system design,
> technical requirements, data model, project CLAUDE.md, prompt
> contract — as the current statement and its reason, rewritten in
> place. The spec's Decisions entry is the record of what it replaced;
> living documents carry no amendment chains.
>
> A decision that changes an established convention — the form or
> source of a value, an accepted rule — also names every artifact that
> encodes the previous answer: fixtures, labelled data, tests,
> prompts, generated configuration. Each gets a **disposition**: it
> moves in this branch, or it gets a queue row. The menu is presented
> only after every decision has a home.

**Placement.** The step sits after Step 0 and before Step 1, not after
Step 1. Step 0's fixes settle the decisions, so the fold has nothing
left to invalidate; and the fold's commits land before the suite run,
so the suite still covers the tree that merges. Placed after Step 1,
the step would make the green suite stale by this skill's own
rationalization row ("a green run only proves the tree it ran on").

**Edit 2 — the Decisions bullet** gains a fourth field:

> *decision / rejected alternative / trigger to revisit / supersedes*

where *supersedes* names the artifact and statement this decision
replaces, or "none".

**Edit 3 — the Amendment path** widens requirement (b) from "every
document that states the amended behavior" to "every artifact that
encodes the amended behavior", and its enumeration is extended past
"spec, PRD, system design" to include fixtures, labelled data, tests,
prompts and generated configuration. Leaving the old enumeration under
the wider noun reads as exhaustive.

**Rationale.** The loop writes one spec per task and specs amend by
addition, so a decision lives in the spec of the task that made it.
Genesis documents are updated only for genesis-level changes; a
decision about a value's form or source falls below that bar and stays
in its spec. The current state of the system is then the fold of every
Decisions section over every spec, and nothing in the loop computes
that fold. In the source project one spec decided that a class of
values is returned as printed; three days later another decided which
source wins per field. Both had Decisions sections. Neither named the
labelled data, which still held the previous answers, so the labels
encoded a superseded convention for five more days and produced thirty
of thirty-three mismatches. By the end the project held 23 specs, 17
analysis notes, 4 findings and 8 decision records, and a queue row had
been opened to consolidate "the latest logic and why it is the
latest", because no document held it. Step 0b makes that fold a
per-task cost instead of a periodic reconstruction.

**This release applies the improvement to itself.**
`docs/testing/pressure-scenarios/change-01-decisions-section.md`
encodes the three-field Decisions form and
`docs/process-map.md` restates the Amendment rule; both move in the
same commit as the decision that supersedes them.

## Improvement E — external preconditions are proven before the first task

**Absorbs** source Changes 19 and 20, the latter narrowed.

**Target:** `skills/writing-plans/SKILL.md` Plan Document Header;
`commands/start-task.md` Phases 1 and 4; the split note in
`skills/finishing-a-branch/post-loop-paths.md`.

**Edit 1 — the plan section**, inside the header template, after
Global Constraints:

> ## Preconditions
>
> [Every external resource a task step needs — a credential, an
> endpoint, a dataset snapshot, a second process such as a conversion
> engine — with the one command that proves it is live now. "none" is
> a valid entry.]

**Edit 2 — `/start-task` Phase 4**, before the first task:

> Run every precondition command from the plan header. A failed
> precondition stops the phase: renew the resource, reorder the plan
> so the tasks that need it come last, or split them into a later
> queue row — before any task starts. This stop is separate from the
> circuit breaker below: the circuit breaker fires on a failure
> *during* execution, this one prevents work accumulating behind a
> dependency that is already dead.

**Edit 3 — `/start-task` Phase 1**, where the queue row is first read:

> If the row names a parked branch carried over from an earlier row,
> re-validate its notes before design: a trial rebase in a throwaway
> worktree, listing the conflicting files, then abort and remove it.
> A note written at the split describes the tree at the split; every
> merge since has moved the tree.

This line sits in Phase 1 and not Phase 0 because Phase 0 is pure git
state and has not read the row.

**Edit 4 — the split note**, beside the Amendment path:

> **Split.** When part of a task moves to a later queue row — a
> recording the environment cannot make, a resource that is down —
> the unfinished work goes on a named branch, never a stash: a stash
> is session-local and conflicts with edits made after it. The split
> is named in three places: the inheriting queue row, the hand-off,
> and an execution amendment to the current spec.

**Rationale.** A plan's task 7 of 9 needed a live third-party
credential. The plan named the probe command inside that task's steps.
Nobody ran it before task 1. The credential was found expired at task
7, after tasks 1 to 6 and 8 were written and committed. The cost was a
stash, then a work-in-progress branch, a scope split, an execution
amendment, and a reorder of two queue rows. The stash then conflicted
with four files edited in between. Separately, a parked branch's note
said "rebase first: row 30 removed eight files"; a later row merged
and touched ten of the branch's files, the note stayed as written, and
a trial rebase showed ten conflicts and two files the carried change
must reach that did not exist when the branch forked.

The trial rebase is placed at resume time, not at every merge. The
information is used when the inheriting row starts, and paying for it
then costs nothing on merges that never precede a resume.

## Improvement F — a queue row names the outcome, not the method

**Absorbs** source Change 16.

**Target:** `commands/design-project.md` Phase 4;
`commands/start-task.md` Phase 6.

This is a clarification of a rule that already exists and was
ambiguous, not a second rule stacked on an ignored one. Phase 4 says
"rows carry no specs or task breakdowns". A named comparator is
neither a spec nor a task breakdown, so the row passed.

**Edit.** Both places where rows are written gain:

> A row states the question or deliverable, the decision it settles,
> its mode, and its dependencies. It does not prescribe the method,
> the comparator, the metric shape, or the file layout: those are the
> design phase's to propose and your human partner's to approve. A
> row that carries a method is a spec that skipped its approval gate.
> Where a hand-off or an earlier session holds a proposal, the row
> cites the note that holds it as an input, and the design phase
> weighs it.

**Rationale.** A queue row for a re-exploration was written from an
in-chat assessment and carried the method in full: a named comparator
per field class, an extra bucket in the report, and containment
matching for one field. The design phase rejected two of the three
after reading the data — one measured a case a validator would make
impossible, and the other would have passed three label values that
appear nowhere in the source. The roadmap's own header says rows are a
queue, not a plan. A row that prescribes the method either binds the
design phase to a decision nobody approved, or costs the design phase
the work of unwinding it. The assessment that motivated the row was
valuable and belonged in a note the row cites.

## Improvement G — "required" is defined by what an absence does

**Absorbs** source Change 23.

**Target:** `skills/persistent-model-discovery/SKILL.md` — a seventh
question and a seventh deliverable item; `commands/design-project.md`
Phase 1c self-check item 5; `templates/prd-app.md` and
`templates/prd-ml.md` Terms guidance.

**Edit 1 — the seventh question.**

> 7. **Acceptance:** for each value the PRD calls required, three
>    answers, in a table with one row per value:
>    - *Absent, what happens?* One of: the record is refused; a named
>      unit is held for a human; the value is null and nothing is
>      held. "Required" without this answer is not a requirement.
>    - *Which unit?* The unit an absence holds is the smallest unit
>      the consumer joins on — never its parent by default. A problem
>      holds the unit it is about and nothing above or beside it.
>    - *Can this source supply it?* Where a data-contact spike ran,
>      cite the instance where the value appears, or state that it
>      never appears. A value the source never carries is not a
>      requirement on this system; it is an integration gap with
>      another source, and it must hold nothing.

The heading "The six questions" becomes "The seven questions", and the
preamble sentence naming four interlocking parts is corrected —
acceptance is a fifth.

**Edit 2 — the PRD self-check clause**, written to stand alone with no
technical-requirements vocabulary, because Phase 1c runs for every
project and most never trigger the TRD:

> "required", "essential" and "mandatory" name exactly the values
> whose absence stops the system from accepting the thing being
> described. A value the business wants but whose absence stops
> nothing gets a different word.

This is a word-choice check, not a design step. It does not ask for an
acceptance table at PRD time; where the TRD triggers, question 7 adds
the unit and the source-supply parts on top.

**Rationale.** A PRD listed required fields at genesis. Later three
more fields were promoted to "essential" because a business goal would
eventually need them. The acceptance gate then blocked any record
missing any essential field. Three facts surfaced only after the
pipeline ran on the pilot set. First, one document class never prints
one of those values at all; every record of that class — five of ten —
blocked on it, and a human opening one could only close it unresolved,
because the value is not in the pack. Second, one record extracted all
53 of its lines correctly and still blocked, because a single doubt
about three duplicate rows held the whole record and its fourteen
links. Third, one field "only ever blocked and never re-asked", so an
earlier row had already demoted it, one field at a time, with no rule.

The question that followed took a day to answer: if an absent
essential field means reject entirely, the automation is not worth
doing; if a field can be essential and still missing, what does the
word mean? The answer was a rebuild of the contract, the merge, the
validator and the outcome report. Question 1 asks what one row is; it
does not ask what one acceptance is. Question 4 asks what consumers
query; it does not ask at which grain they join.

## Improvement H — new behaviour's tests are proven by an executed mutation

**Absorbs** source Change 22, narrowed.

**Target:** `agents/code-reviewer.md` Ground Rules and Build-Mode
Rubric.

**Status: conditional experiment.** Revisit after ten build reviews.
Demote if it finds nothing and costs minutes each time.

**Edit 1 — Ground Rules.** The read-only rule is scoped, not removed.
The agent's tool grant already includes Bash; only the phrase "never
modify files" needs a bound, and the clause about having no Write or
Edit tools stays true and useful:

> Never move HEAD, never modify the working tree — you have no
> Write/Edit tools by design. The one permitted write is a disposable
> copy outside the working tree, used for the mutation check below;
> the repository itself is never touched.

**Edit 2 — the coverage check.** The existing quality bullet is
rewritten from an existence check into an evidence check, rather than
adding a second coverage mechanism beside it:

> **Test coverage of new behavior (HIGH):** for each behavior the spec
> names as new, copy the tree to a scratch location outside the
> working tree, reduce that behavior to identity or remove it, run the
> suite there, and report the mutation with the exact set of tests it
> turned red. A new test that stays green has proven nothing.
>
> **Inconclusive guard:** if the mutation changes *nothing at all* —
> no test fails and no error appears — the copy is probably not the
> code under test. An editable install resolves imports to the
> original package, so the suite runs unmutated. Report **inconclusive
> and say why**; never report "uncovered".
>
> Bound the work to the behaviors the spec names as new. Where the
> suite is too slow to run once per behavior, say so in the report
> rather than skipping silently.

**Rationale.** On one task the reviewer, unprompted, reduced a
placement step to identity and removed a denominator rule in a scratch
copy, and reported that exactly five new tests and one evaluation test
turned red and nothing else moved. That is the only check in the loop
that proves a test-first cycle was honest after the fact, and it took
minutes. The existing rule asks only whether each new function has a
test, which a tautological test satisfies. Watching a test fail is
required during execution but leaves no artifact a reviewer can read.

## Improvement I — a survey of outside solutions adopts only what the evidence needs

**Absorbs** the guard of source Change 18. The genesis trigger of that
change does not ship; see Deferred.

**Target:** `skills/writing-comparative-reports/SKILL.md`, Part 2.

**Edit.** Part 2's preamble is widened to admit surveys — it currently
presupposes a profile's handoff sections, which an external-solutions
survey does not have. Part 2's existing labelling move is extended
with documented-versus-marketing, rather than adding a parallel
labelling item. One new move is added:

> **Where the instances are products or published methods, not the
> project's own artifacts:** the report carries two more sections.
> *Mapping to this project*: for each signal or mechanism found,
> whether this project's own evidence shows it is needed, citing the
> instance that needs it. *What we would not adopt and why*. An item
> enters the requirements only through the mapping's cited instance.
> The survey proposes; the project's evidence decides. A finding with
> no instance behind it is recorded as an open question, never as a
> requirement.

**Rationale.** A survey of twenty commercial products and the related
literature ran four weeks after the system had been designed, built
and evaluated. It found four gaps the design had never considered, two
of which would have been requirements at genesis. It also confirmed
one design choice with evidence the design had lacked: of twenty
products none publishes a calibration, and two papers show the
self-reported measure collapsing at practical thresholds. Before the
survey, that question had been answered from the system's own logic
("redundant") and the four gaps stayed unknown.

The guard answers the question the survey raised: how is replicating
without understanding avoided? A market mechanism is adopted only when
the project's own evidence shows the need for it, by instance, and the
ruled-out section is required, so understanding is written down where
imitation would otherwise hide.

## Known budget breaches, untouched by this release

`skills/creating-skills/SKILL.md` sets the budget: always-loaded under
150 words, frequently loaded under 200 lines. Two files are over it,
